Onion Storage System
1. Project Overview

I engineered an Improved Onion Storage System designed to drastically reduce post-harvest losses caused by sprouting, rotting, and moisture loss — a major source of revenue loss for farmers relying on traditional, inefficient storage methods. The core goal was to replace passive storage with a highly regulated, controlled-environment system that actively extends shelf life.

2. Development & Technical Challenges

The system was built around precise environmental control: I configured it to maintain optimal curing temperatures (25–30°C) before transitioning to long-term storage levels (0–2°C), while strictly managing relative humidity at 65–70% to inhibit fungal growth and prevent premature sprouting.

The key technical challenge was preventing localized damp hotspots, where rot typically begins. To solve this, I integrated a forced-air ventilation system that continuously circulates air through the storage structures, actively removing excess heat and respiratory moisture generated by the onions themselves. Combined with a calculated pre-storage curing phase to dry the outer skins, this uniform airflow framework extends the crop's shelf life up to 8 months — directly supporting market supply stability and reducing farmer revenue loss.

On the fabrication side, I modelled the storage unit's structural panels in SolidWorks, designing interlocking slot joints specifically for laser-cutting fabrication. Working with MDF for the first time required calibrating the laser's kerf carefully to achieve accurate press-fit tolerances between panels — a materials and manufacturing challenge distinct from the metal and polymer work in my other projects.

3. Learning Outcome

This project expanded my design-to-production skill set using SolidWorks and precision manufacturing techniques. I gained hands-on experience designing interlocking, laser-cut structural panels, and working with MDF for the first time taught me to evaluate its machinability and flat structural integrity while calibrating fabrication tolerances for a clean press-fit. Ultimately, this project gave me a practical, end-to-end understanding of how to take a digital design from CAD to a functional physical prototype.
