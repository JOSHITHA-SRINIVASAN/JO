Project Overview

As part of the Adastra Autonomous Rover Team, I was responsible for designing and fabricating the rover's science cabinet — a precision-engineered enclosure built to house and protect the rover's scientific instruments during competition. The cabinet needed to secure complex, sensitive equipment under field conditions while staying lightweight and compact enough to fit within the rover's overall chassis envelope. It played a direct role in the successful execution of the team's science tasks in competition.

Development & Technical Challenges

The cabinet was designed end-to-end in SolidWorks, following DFMA principles from the earliest concept stage. Material selection was a key early decision: SS304 at 0.8 mm thickness for its corrosion resistance, low mass, and compatibility with laser cutting and bending.

The main design challenge was balancing two competing constraints: maximizing usable internal space for mounting multiple scientific instruments, while keeping the structure rigid and secure enough to survive vibration, handling, and field conditions during competition. This meant iterating on panel layout and mounting points until the cabinet achieved both efficient space utilization and secure, stable instrument mounting — without adding unnecessary weight or complexity.

Learning Outcome

I developed proficiency in SolidWorks Sheet Metal — working with bend allowances, K-factors, and flat-pattern generation for precision sheet metal enclosures. I gained practical material knowledge of SS304, evaluating its structural rigidity, corrosion resistance, and durability against the demands of rover operating conditions.

Project Overview

I engineered an Improved Onion Storage System designed to drastically reduce post-harvest losses caused by sprouting, rotting, and moisture loss — a major source of revenue loss for farmers relying on traditional storage. The core goal was to replace passive storage with a highly regulated, controlled-environment system that actively extends shelf life.

Development & Technical Challenges

The system was built around precise environmental control: configured to maintain optimal curing temperatures (25–30°C) before transitioning to long-term storage levels (0–2°C), while strictly managing relative humidity at 65–70% to inhibit fungal growth. I integrated a forced-air ventilation system that continuously circulates air through the storage structures, actively removing excess heat and respiratory moisture — extending shelf life to 8 months.

On the fabrication side, I modelled the structural panels in SolidWorks, designing interlocking slot joints specifically for laser-cutting. Working with MDF for the first time required calibrating the laser's kerf carefully to achieve accurate press-fit tolerances between panels.

Project Overview

I designed a Microbial Fuel Cell (MFC) — a bio-electrochemical system that generates usable electricity by harnessing the metabolic activity of bacteria digesting agricultural waste. The product converts an otherwise low-value waste stream into a continuous, low-power electrical current through a closed-loop, three-chamber system.

Development & Technical Challenges

The core engineering challenge was designing a structure that kept three distinct chemical environments working together without contaminating one another. The anode chamber is sealed and kept completely oxygen-free, where exoelectrogenic bacteria digest agricultural waste, stripping electrons from organic matter. A Proton Exchange Membrane acts as a selective gatekeeper, allowing protons to pass while strictly blocking oxygen. The cathode chamber completes the circuit, producing only harmless water as a byproduct.

The main design difficulty was maintaining strict fluid and gas isolation between the anaerobic and aerobic sides while positioning electrodes close enough for efficient electron harvesting — two requirements that pull the design in opposite directions.

How it works

Bacteria in the sealed anode chamber digest agri-waste → electrons travel through external wire as current → protons pass through PEM → combine with O₂ in cathode → H₂O only byproduct.

Learning Outcome

This project let me bridge biology and engineering — translating micro-organism metabolism into a predictable, usable electrical current. I gained practical experience in structural architecture and environmental isolation, integrating an anaerobic anode chamber, a PEM, and an aerobic cathode chamber into a single cohesive unit.

Project Overview

As Mechanical Subsystem Co-Lead for Adastra's student-built autonomous rover, I was responsible for engineering and directing core mechanical assemblies — focusing on maximizing mobility, structural integrity, and reliability across varied terrain. Leadership & Outcome

Coordinated mechanical development cycles, aligned design choices with broader team requirements, and ensured functional reliability under field conditions. The team achieved First Runner-Up at IRC 2026 — validating the rover as a competitive system against international university teams.

Project Overview

I engineered the rover's wheels from the ground up — maximizing mobility, traction, and durability across varied and unpredictable terrain. Rather than relying on traditional rigid wheel construction, I used advanced additive manufacturing techniques paired with TPU tires, allowing wheel stiffness to be structurally tuned through geometry rather than material thickness alone.

Iteration 1 → 2

Wave Tread: Strong forward thrust and lateral grip with self-cleaning geometry — but testing revealed uneven point-load distribution causing excessive sinkage in deep, loose sand.

Transverse Rib: Redesigned for flotation and propulsion efficiency in fine soils. Widened terrain contact area distributes weight uniformly, eliminating sinkage. The wide ribs mechanically scoop and push soil backward, generating higher forward reaction force.

Learning Outcome

Extensive hands-on DFAM experience — bridging complex CAD models with physical production using flexible TPU filaments. I developed strong proficiency in slicer software, tuning retraction speed and print temperature to solve stringing and poor bed adhesion. I learned to balance TPU's impact absorption and structural flexibility against its fabrication constraints, strategically tuning infill density and geometry to engineer a high-performance, field-ready rover wheel.
